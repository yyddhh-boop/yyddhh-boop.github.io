About

Intelligence, knowledge, and information

My current research program lies at the intersection of AI for Science, the philosophy of mathematics and science, and information theory. I am interested in both technical systems and foundational questions: how scientific agents represent problems, what makes a concept usable and communicable, and which kinds of information matter for a given task.

A central goal is to develop an operational and structured account of information—one that does not treat information as a single context-free number, but asks what can be extracted from a system, through which probes or operations, at what cost, and for which purpose.

Research

Current directions

Three connected directions organized around scientific intelligence, conceptual foundations, and task-relevant information.

AI for Science (AI4Science)

Developing AI agents and computational methods for scientific reasoning, mathematical exploration, literature synthesis, model building, hypothesis generation, and human–AI collaboration.

Philosophy of Mathematics and Science

Studying mathematics and science as practices through which humans and artificial systems stabilize concepts, construct explanations, test claims, and coordinate reliable reasoning.

Developing a New Information Theory

Building an operational theory of structured and task-dependent information: what can be learned from a system, which probes make it accessible, how much inquiry costs, and how the answer changes with the task.
